# RecallReady · Submission video script

Recording edit v3 · November 8, 2026 · silent timeline 2:50

## Creative concept

**Three orders. One recall. Three different decisions.**

The story opens with a decision the viewer can try to make. It reveals the source conflict, demonstrates three distinct order outcomes, then proves one deliberately approved sandbox refund and the same attempt returned on repeat. The intended audience is a small online seller or support operator.

All eight original app scenes were captured on November 8, 2026. Four corrected-layout pickups were subsequently captured against the same completed transaction. The original human approvals are retained. The owner will generate the narration manually in ElevenLabs; no voiceover was generated here.

## Narration and shots

**Narration:**

Three power bank orders. Same brand. Similar titles. One recall. Would you refund all three? One matches. One has a different model. One is missing the evidence. The challenge is deciding which is which.

### 0:18–0:32 · A specific person and job

**Show:** Brief landing-page identity, then actual merchant workbench. Keep the sandbox badge visible. Overlay: FOR SMALL SELLERS & SUPPORT TEAMS.

**Narration:**

RecallReady helps small online sellers and support teams move from an official recall to a documented decision. Every case connects three things: the source, the order, and the merchant’s authority to act.

### 0:32–0:59 · AI meets contradictory source evidence

**Show:** Actual CPSC source and a fresh live Gemini extraction. Open AI details briefly to show provider/response metadata. Highlight exact PB0003 paragraph and PB003 caption; show the user's deliberate rule review. Do not overlay a fake model stream.

**Narration:**

Here is the official CPSC notice. Gemini extracts model details and remedy instructions with supporting quotes. Our server validates them against the saved source. But the description says P B zero zero zero three; the caption says P B zero zero three. We preserve that disagreement. The operator reviews the exact rule. AI cannot authorize a refund.

### 0:59–1:24 · Three outcomes the judge can verify

**Show:** Use actual order selection/search: exact RR-001, near-match RR-002, missing-model RR-003. Show explicit reasons and disabled refund controls for RR-002/RR-003. Return to RR-001. Overlay: MATCH / DIFFERENT MODEL / NEEDS EVIDENCE.

**Narration:**

Now watch the three orders separate. The exact model is a confirmed match. The different model is not matched to this notice. The missing model stays in review, and its financial action is disabled. The workbench makes the reason visible. A similar product title never establishes eligibility. An exact product match is only the first gate.

### 1:24–1:58 · Show the real money boundary

**Show:** Fresh genuine sandbox capture prepared before this take. Show source-backed match, separate manufacturer remedy and merchant policy, then immutable approval with USD49.00/capture ID. User clicks checkbox and approves. Observe worker result; show authoritative completed status/refund ID. Brief overlay: SOURCE → RULE → APPROVAL → PAYPAL GET. Disclose any wait cuts.

**Narration:**

Next comes the merchant’s independent courtesy policy. Belkin’s manufacturer remedy remains a separate follow-up; RecallReady does not file that claim. For this owned sandbox payment, the operator reviews forty-nine U S dollars and explicitly approves. The approval binds the amount, capture, and evidence versions. The worker rechecks them before sending the refund. It then reads PayPal’s refund record to verify the capture association, amount, and actual status. Completed means verified.

### 1:58–2:19 · The second request is the signature proof

**Show:** Hold completed refund and ID. Click Re-submit this approval. Hold same attempt ID and existing-attempt message. Show concise audit proof. A synthetic timeout may be a separate inset marked SIMULATION only if useful footage exists; no fake live fault.

**Narration:**

Now repeat the request. The same attempt returns. No second refund is sent. If the outcome is uncertain, RecallReady shows uncertainty and checks provider records. An uncertain response never becomes permission to resend. The receipt keeps the financial result and its source evidence together.

### 2:19–2:35 · Substantive implementation in a product

**Show:** Brief AG Grid search and filters; one compact architecture visual. Overlay actual observed counts: 111 unit/component, 26 PostgreSQL integration (mocked provider), 6 browser. Show public MIT repository/run instructions. Do not claim a clean-checkout test until performed.

**Narration:**

AG Grid makes the order evidence inspectable. PostgreSQL preserves approvals and the durable execution record. The public MIT repository includes setup instructions, tests, and the verified sandbox proof. Reviewers can explore an isolated simulation without payment credentials.

### 2:35–2:50 · Honest scope and memorable payoff

**Show:** Return to three case outcomes and real refund proof. Final lockup: RECALLREADY / THE RIGHT ORDER. THE RIGHT EVIDENCE. YOUR DECISION. Keep disclosure: SYNTHETIC ORDERS · REAL AI · PAYPAL SANDBOX.

**Narration:**

Synthetic orders. Real source extraction. PayPal sandbox transactions. Merchant demand is our next validation step. RecallReady: from a confusing notice to an approved, verified refund. The right order. The right evidence. Your decision.

## Recommendation

Make the repeat-request test the signature moment. Spend screen time on observable behavior, not a list of APIs. The intended message is **the operator can trace the decision and retain control of the money**. The quality of the demo can improve the entry; it cannot guarantee a prize.
